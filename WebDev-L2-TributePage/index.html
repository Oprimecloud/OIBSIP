The Genesis of JavaScript

In May 1995, the web was a static, document-driven platform. Netscape Communications realized they needed a lightweight scripting language to make the web dynamic and accessible to non-professional programmers. They hired Brendan Eich, who, in a legendary sprint of just ten days, prototyped a language initially called Mocha, later renamed to LiveScript, and finally to JavaScript.

Eich's design choices—combining C-style syntax with first-class functions and prototype-based object orientation—created a highly flexible language that would eventually power everything from simple animations to massive single-page applications.

"If I had done class-based inheritance, it would just have been a terribly poorly thought out rip-off of C++ or Java. I liked the flexibility of prototypes." — Brendan Eich

Key Milestones

Born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Received his degree in mathematics and computer science.

1995

Recruited by Netscape Communications and created JavaScript in just 10 days.

1998

Co-founded the Mozilla project, building the open-source foundation for Firefox.

2015

Founded Brave Software, pioneering privacy-focused browsing and digital advertising.
